# A topic's page

A topic is a market, technology, course, place or matter the user returns to.
It needs at least three sources spanning fourteen days and two records, or an
explicit user request to remember/learn it. Generic words, vendors and projects
under another name stay on the existing record. Never create a topic about
another person's health, even at the user's request.

Search every supplied alias in every authorized source, like an org's domains:
local mail, chats, coding sessions and notebook pages. Use the supplied topic
candidate evidence and original source IDs; a page is context, not a new source.
A copied digest is not independent evidence. Keep the English alias in the slug
and the title in the owner's language. Merge exact shared aliases with
`co rem merge KEPT OTHER`; this preserves the old path as an alias.

Every section is present in this order; unsupported sections say `Unknown`.
Every factual sentence has a claim number `[n]` into `Sources`.

```markdown
# Thermal storage
- Also known as: heat batteries; heat storage

## What it is
Unknown

## Where you stand
Unknown

## People
Unknown

## Orgs
Unknown

## Projects
Unknown

## Timeline
Unknown

## Open threads
Unknown

## Uncertainties
Unknown

## Sources
Unknown
```

Lead with two or three cited sentences about the user's current position.
People, Orgs and Projects hold relative links, each with the sentence that
establishes its relevance and its original citation. Write each link once here;
the reader supplies "linked from" on the other end. Never duplicate whole pages.
Sessions and skills are counts only, never notebook links. Timeline is dated
changes, not extraction or investigation dates. Open threads are current requests
or agreed obligations, not tasks invented to settle historical uncertainty.

An alias-only or ambiguous match is not a relationship. Propose a yes/no link
review with its sentence; rejected matches remain excluded. Do not write those
links before acceptance. Accepted and rejected review decisions are supplied as
evidence. Distinct subjects with merely similar spellings are not aliases.

Health, family, personal money and legal/immigration topics carry `[personal]`
on the title line. Preserve it on every rewrite; all their content is behind the
reader privacy curtain. Never put private topics or their facts in handoff or
exports. If health evidence concerns somebody else, refuse the topic.
