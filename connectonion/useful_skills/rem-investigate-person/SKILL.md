# Investigating a person

Resolve names, companies, public role and links across mail and outside sources;
a name match alone is not identity. Lead with who they are and their value to
the user. Follow the appended person shape; Open threads is mandatory.

## Identity and mail

- Mailbox-owner addresses are the user's, never the subject's. Email/Handles
  take only their own sending addresses; put names/aliases/scripts in Also known
  as unless denied, companies in Company. Coverage mailbox owner is the user's
  page; follow rem-owner-page.
- A role in a list the user writes about someone else is that person's, not
  the user's. Roles require a statement by/about the subject.
- Another same-person page: flag in Uncertainties; do not merge.
- Project documents naming them are the owner's record; contact details there
  alone are not the person's own.
- Read `investigation:facts` first into cited Facts. Read Signature/Calendar and
  signatures for role/company/office/location/time zone; date changes and qualify
  phones. Calendar context is not contact detail.
- Derive Unknown time zone only from unambiguous established city/country,
  e.g. `Australia/Sydney (from location) [n]`, citing the location's source.
  Ambiguous location or several time zones stays Unknown; do not guess a fixed UTC offset.
- Domain/student affiliation proves no employment. Link matching organisation
  in the relationship, citing mail. `[attachment]` text carries terms; cite its
  ID, report unreadability.
- Mail gives identity/commitments; sessions give intent. Cite both when one
  states and another implies; show disagreements.
- Check all topics' asks/promises before Nothing open. Questions promise no
  deadline. Invites/logistics/arrival/empty forwards/samples prove no completion.
  In groups preserve asker/decider/debtor; a group reply may close the same ask.
- Reread Comparison scope originals by exact Provider thread; they are not new contact.

Insight connects evidence: stakes, dated change, repeated communication pattern
or labelled inference; never copy illustrative facts.
