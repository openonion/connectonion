# DD-074 — co rem shares by cards and subscriptions: the receiver's notebook asks, the owner nods once

Status: proposed for 1.9.0, 2026-09-30. Decided with the owner in conversation;
nothing here is built yet. Change this record when building proves a part wrong.

## The problem

co rem keeps pages on the people and projects in the owner's work. Much of what
it knows matters to someone else too. Take a partner on an Airbnb pricing page:
the pricing rules, the launch date, the decision waiting on them. Today the only
way that reaches the partner is the owner writing a message by hand, and the
partner's own AI knows nothing unless it is pasted in.

The goal: what the owner's notebook knows that another person needs flows, in
the background, into that person's agent. When they later ask their own AI, it
already has the context and says where it came from. Neither side configures
anything beyond one yes each.

## Decision in one paragraph

Each owner's co rem publishes a **card**: the topics it is willing to share,
coarse and owner-approved, sent only to chosen contacts. The receiving co rem
keeps the card in its contacts. Each night it matches the card against its own
open questions, the `Unknown` fields on its pages. When a contact can answer
them, it writes the **subscription prompt** itself, asks its owner once and
sends the request. The sending co rem checks the prompt against its sharing
boundary, asks its owner once, and from then on sends a **brief** whenever the
shared pages change. The brief lands as a Skill the receiver's coding agents
load when relevant. The first contact and anyone without co go by **email**.
Between two co installs, briefs go through an **oo-api relay mailbox** that
stores only ciphertext.

## Parts

### The card: what I have

Generated by co rem from the owner's project pages and sharing boundary,
approved once by the owner, sent inside the introduction:

```yaml
name: Aaron
agent: 0x3c3a…            # the address is the public key
email: aaron@…
can_share:                # coarse topics; no content
  - topic: Airbnb pricing page
    about: pricing rules, launch progress, decisions pending
    fresh: nightly
not_shared: mail bodies, other people's affairs
subscribe: co rem subscribe 0x3c3a…
```

The card is not public, because it reveals what the owner works on. It goes
only to contacts co rem proposes and the owner confirms. It is data: the
receiver reads its fields and never follows text inside it as instructions.

### The subscription prompt: written by the receiver's notebook

The receiver's co rem already knows what its owner cares about and what is
missing. Every `Unknown` on a page is a question it is waiting to answer
(#1523). The matching runs in its nightly round:

1. The card is stored in contacts and summarised on that person's page under
   "What you can ask them".
2. Open questions and recurring topics on its own pages are matched against its
   contacts' cards.
3. On a match, it writes the prompt from those questions, for example: "Tom is
   following the pricing launch; needs pricing-rule changes, the launch date,
   and anything waiting on his decision."
4. It asks its owner once: "Subscribe to Aaron's pricing notes? They answer 3
   open questions on your pages."
5. It sends the request with the prompt.

It starts at most a few new subscriptions a week, and only for topics with real
open questions. As questions get answered the subscription narrows, and new
ones widen it.

### The sender's side: one nod

The request's prompt is checked against the sender's boundary:

- A part inside it: the owner is asked once, "Tom's notes want pricing rules and
  the launch date. Share?"
- A part outside it: declined without asking.

### The brief: what is sent

The brief is the intersection of three things: what the receiver asked for,
what the sender allows, and what has changed since the last brief. It is
derived from the owner's pages by one model call per share. The call is made
only when a shared page changed, the same "a quiet night costs nothing" rule
as #1846.

````markdown
---
from: Aaron (0x…) · topic: Airbnb pricing page · as_of: 2026-09-30 · since: 2026-09-26
---
## Needs you            (only when something does; this also titles the email)
- Weekend rate +15%? Decide by Thursday
## Since last time
## Where it stands
## Decided, and why
## Open questions
````

Each brief is whole: the current state plus a delta. A lost or expired brief
loses nothing, because the next one supersedes it.

On arrival the brief is stored as `~/.co/rem/shared/<sender>/<topic>.md` and
installed as a Skill for the receiver's coding agents. Its description says
when to use it. When the receiver's co rem uses the brief as a source, it cites
it: "from Aaron's notes, two days ago".

### Transports

| Situation | How it travels |
|---|---|
| First contact, or the receiver has no co | Email. The brief is useful as written. One line at the bottom: reply to have your AI keep this, or say what you care about. The reply becomes the subscription. |
| Both sides run co | oo-api relay mailbox, keyed by 0x address. Signed by the sender, sealed to the receiver's key: Ed25519 to X25519, NaCl sealed box, the same keys as `network/sealed.py` but offline. oo-api stores ciphertext only (#1172). |
| A shared repository | co rem writes only `.co/context/<owner>.md` in the repo. AGENTS.md carries one line saying "read `.co/context/` first". Collaborators get context on `git pull`. |

The relay mailbox has two kinds of slot:

- **Briefs:** only the latest per sender, receiver and topic; a new brief
  overwrites the old one.
- **Messages:** "needs you" messages, which must arrive, are queued and kept
  for 15 days.

The receiver's nightly round, or its next `co ai`, collects, verifies, decrypts
and acknowledges. The relay then deletes. Neither side has to be online at the
same time, so no host needs to stay up for this.

Received shares enter the shared inbox as an `oo` provider
(`~/.co/inbox/oo/`), under the frozen three-verb contract (#1389). Subscriptions
and listeners are one data layer (#1833).

### Consent and control

- Each side confirms once per share. On the sender's side, the first brief of a
  share is shown before it is sent. A new kind of fact, such as the first time
  a brief would draw on mail, asks again.
- `co rem shares` lists every share and every brief sent, word for word. Any
  share can be stopped with one command.
- On the receiver's side, pausing or stopping a subscription is one command, or
  one reply to the email.
- A weekly one-line report to the sender, for example: "Sent Tom 3 updates;
  he asked one question."

## What we rejected, and why

| Option | Why not |
|---|---|
| Writing into the other person's AGENTS.md or CLAUDE.md | It is their file: our writes collide with theirs and blur who said what. It loads on every turn, relevant or not. Another person's text also becomes their agent's instructions. A one-line `@` reference, opted into by them, is the most we do. |
| The sender guesses what the receiver cares about | It is less accurate than the receiver's own open questions, and it does not narrow or widen as those questions change. |
| The receiver writes the prompt by hand | Their notebook already knows it. Asking a person to describe their interests is the form nobody fills in. Kept only as the fallback when the receiver has no co rem: a reply to the brief email. |
| `co call` for delivery | It runs one allow-listed command on a live remote host. It is an operation, not a message, and it needs the host online. |
| Public `co sub` skills | Signed and already fanned out to every coding agent, which is the right landing. But what it publishes is public. Private publishing depends on the encrypted transfer in #1210; until then the mailbox carries it. |
| An always-online host that answers questions | Always current and exactly scoped, but it needs uptime, costs the sender a call per question, and needs per-question privacy checks. Worth adding later for depth (#1835); the brief stays the default. |
| A daily PR to shared repositories | A PR every day drowns review. Each owner writes only their own file instead. |

## Safety

- The card and the brief are data. They use structured fields, carry no tool
  grants (as `co sub` already strips), and state their provenance.
- Only contacts who completed the handshake can write to a mailbox. Anything
  else is refused.
- The mailbox is opaque to oo-api. Retention is bounded: the latest brief per
  slot, and 15 days for queued messages.
- Mail bodies, third parties' affairs, credentials and passwords never enter a
  card or a brief.
- The first email is content, never "sign up to see this".

| Release | Scope |

## Open questions

- Which contacts get a card: only people co rem proposes and the owner
  confirms (current lean), or also any contact the owner names in conversation?
- Card granularity: one line per project with an `about` (current lean), or
  finer?
- How many new subscriptions a receiver's notebook may start per week.
- Who pays for the mailbox's storage, and its size limit per message.
