# The page was talking about you

The redesigned co rem reader had been out for an hour when the review came
back with two things, and both were about who the page is for.

The first was a column. The People sheet was built so you could see at a
glance who is waiting on you. At 1440 pixels, the width most laptops show it
at, that column was the one cut off at the right edge. Every other fact fitted:
company, role, email, phone, last contact, mail count. The one you open the
sheet for was the one you had to scroll sideways to find. We had laid the
columns out in the order the investigation writes them, not the order a reader
needs them. *What's open* now sits next to the name, the status column is gone
(the moon beside each name already says it), and the rest are clamped so the
sheet fits. On a narrower screen the sheet scrolls inside itself, and the cut
edge is shaded so a hidden column does not look like a missing one.

The second was a word. The investigation writes about "the user": "the user
has not signed it", "How the user writes to them". That is right for the
model, which needs to know whose notebook it is writing. It is wrong for the
person reading their own notebook, who is that user. A page about your own
relationships kept referring to you in the third person, like a case file.

We did not change the pages. The Markdown stays as the investigation wrote it,
and Copy Markdown still copies it word for word. The reader changes it as it
draws: "the user has not signed" becomes "you have not signed", "the user's"
becomes "your", and the verb that follows is made to agree. The tricky cases
were the ones where the next word only looks like a verb: "owes the user its
proposal" has to stay "owes you its proposal", not "owes you it proposal".

The lesson: a design is checked against whoever ends up reading it. The
column order suited the writer and the pronoun suited the model; the reader
needed both changed.
