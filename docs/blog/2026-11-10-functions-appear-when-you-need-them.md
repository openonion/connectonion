# Functions appear when you need them

*2026-11-10*

Avery opened Today and counted 63 things he could click before he had read a
single line. Every one of them was useful: hand this item to Codex, talk it
over with Claude, switch the reply to Lark, read the Chinese gist, open the
original thread, approve seventeen things at once. Together they said nothing.
His verdict was short: it has no hierarchy, and it is overwhelming.

The tempting fix is subtraction. Three earlier directions tried it and each
one bought calm by deleting a function or by drawing a button that did
nothing. The rule we kept instead is the one he gave: a function shows up when
you need it and is absent when you do not; nothing may be removed.

So every control on five pages got a level. At rest a page shows one focus and
one primary action. On Today that is the count, the fuse of who is late, the
front card's letter and its green Send. An item's Hand to Codex, Talk it over,
Expand and done arrive when the pointer or the keyboard reaches that line. The
letter's writing tools arrive when you are in the letter; its gist, the reason
it was written that way, the other channels and the thread wait in its More.
The lists behind Today and approve-all wait in the page's More. What REM did
overnight folds under the queue, its one summary sentence still on screen.
Today now rests on 24 controls, sidebar included.

Records had a second problem: they had turned into to-do lists. Mara's page
listed a dozen open topics with buttons. A record is not a place to act, so those
became one quiet line that links to Waiting on you, and the page opens with one
sentence about where things stand with her instead. The organisations list
became what a CRM would show: Fernhill Labs, its course, its team, indented,
with how many people you know there and when you last heard from any of them.

That last column found a bug. Fernhill Labs said its last contact was a month
ago while Mara had written three days ago. The organisation's own page date
only moves when REM re-reads it; its people's mail moves every night. The row
now takes the newest exchange of anyone there, and a test with invented pages
failed on the old reader before the fix went in.

What we measured, per page, before and after: controls visible at rest, which
are moved behind a trigger, and that every button behind a trigger still does
its job when clicked. The numbers are in the pull request.
