# When your own name sat across the desk

Before cutting 1.9.7rc29, I wanted to verify the new two-screen Waiting on You list against a notebook shaped like a synced second machine — Markdown pages intact under `people/` and `orgs/`, `.state/rem.db` present, and no `.state/map.json` left over from the original onboarding run. I opened `#view=open` expecting to see Mara Ostrowski and Fernhill Labs in the counterparty column. Instead, the top obligation solemnly informed me that the person waiting on the reply was Casey Lin — the notebook's own owner.

At first glance it looked like a bug in the new list template, as if the counterparty column had swapped `from` and `to`. So I checked the Markdown on disk. Nothing was backwards: `people/owner-casey.md` had recorded the obligation from the owner's side, just as `people/mara-ostrowski.md` had recorded it from Mara's. On the primary laptop, `.state/map.json` named `"owner": {"record": "people/owner-casey.md"}`, so the reader knew to look past the owner's page and credit Mara. On the synced copy without `map.json`, `owner_record()` shrugged and returned an empty string. Once `REM.owner` went blank, the reader treated the owner's own biography page like just another external client, AND counted the same obligation twice — once under Mara and once under Casey.

Every fixture test we had ever written built `map.json` alongside the pages, so that silent blank never surfaced in CI. Yet the answer was sitting in plain English inside the Markdown the whole time: line four of `people/owner-casey.md` literally read `How we know them: This is the owner's page.` alongside the owner's email address.

Teaching `owner_record()` to fall back to that page marker and the configured owner addresses — and teaching `dedupOpenEntries()` to merge matching obligations across linked pages — made both ghosts vanish at once. A derived state file is a cache, not an identity document; when the Markdown already knows whose desk this is, the reader shouldn't need a sidecar file to recognize the person sitting in the chair.
