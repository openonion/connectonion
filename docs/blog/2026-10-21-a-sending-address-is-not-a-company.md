# A sending address is not a company

The owner's company page is now written from the owner's own sent mail. To
know which organisation that is, the first version reused an older rule: a
domain the owner sends from is theirs. That rule was built for one job,
keeping the owner's company off the list of counterparties, and there it did
little harm. Pointed at the new page, it was wrong. A student sends from their
university. An employee sends from their employer. Under that rule either
would get a page claiming the owner as its founder, written from every message
they had sent.

A domain now counts as the owner's company only when the owner's page names it
as their Company or Signing entity. A project can establish it too, but only
when the owner also sends mail from that domain. A project for a pilot customer
names the customer as its organisation, and a project's website is often
github.com or vercel.app. Neither of those is the owner's company. A university,
webmail or hosting-platform domain never counts, even when a page names it by
mistake. An employer the owner writes from is an
ordinary organisation page again, like any other the owner deals with.

The company page also stops taking all of the owner's sent mail. It keeps a
message only if it went to someone on the company's domains, or if it names
the company, one of its domains, or one of its products. Everything else is
dropped before it becomes evidence or has its attachments read, and the page
says how many messages it kept of how many it saw. A product name of two or
three letters has to appear capitalised, as written. A word like "go" would
otherwise have brought in half the mailbox.

Two older rules had the same mistake in them. The queue left the owner's
company out entirely, and tidy archived its unwritten page. Both now treat it
as a page we write.
