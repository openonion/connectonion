# What three synthetic test contacts never told us

Every unit and browser test we had written for the `co rem` reader was green. In our test fixtures, Alice owed Bob a reply, one organisation had two members, and the knowledge graph held four tidy nodes that fit comfortably in the middle of the screen. Looking at those clean screenshots, it was easy to believe the interface was finished. Then we copied an actual working notebook of two thousand four hundred and sixty-seven pages into an isolated sandbox, booted a loopback server with outbound mail stubbed out, and clicked through our own real morning queue.

Within thirty seconds, the illusion of green tests fell apart in ways no three-row fixture could have predicted. Selecting two overdue items in the batch bar lit up a bright Viridian action button that did nothing at all when clicked, because our tiny DOM helper had passed `null` straight into `setAttribute("disabled", null)`, quietly turning a cleared boolean into the literal string `"null"` and freezing nine primary controls while leaving them styled as active. Worse, opening an item that carried a sensitive tax-compliance quote showed a polite rose curtain over the headline while printing the exact verbatim quote underneath it in plain text, because the quote extractor had sliced the quotation out of the sentence before the privacy tag at the tail of the line was ever inspected. Switching to our new ultramarine skin at night turned four hundred elements white-on-white because two CSS selectors of equal specificity fought over the canvas, and typing the single letter `"a"` into the search box froze the main thread for a full second while the browser eagerly constructed sixteen thousand `<mark>` tags for folded rows nobody had asked to expand.

What surprised us most was not any single bug, but why our disciplined test suite had missed all of them together. Synthetic fixtures test the cases you remember to invent, and engineers naturally invent tidy, well-behaved nouns. Real notebooks are unruly: thirteen hundred contacts where twelve hundred are one-line stubs, obligations written in mixed English and Chinese with nested quotes and GitHub issue numbers, phones held vertically at `390px` while a bottom sheet slides under a higher `z-index` navigation rail, and Linux servers clicking a button that assumed a macOS Keychain existed. Every assumption we had made about scale, specificity, and text shape had been silently flattered by our miniature test data.

Instead of treating the audit as a one-off bug bash, we turned the sandbox itself into a repeatable evolution loop. Cloning a live notebook into a mode-`0700` scratch home with pre-warmed caches and stubbed side effects lets headless Chromium exercise real data density, contrast ratios across every skin and theme, three-level privacy redaction, and sub-hundred-millisecond interaction budgets in seconds without ever touching a real file or sending a real email. Synthetic tests prove that code does what you intended; only rendering the messy weight of your own real life proves that the room actually holds up when you move into it.
