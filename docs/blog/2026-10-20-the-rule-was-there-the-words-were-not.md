# The rule was there; the words were not

The first fix taught the page writer to quote. Pages got better on average and stayed uneven: on identical evidence, one rerun quoted a person's mail twenty-seven times and the next three times. A rule a model follows sometimes is a preference.

So the floor moved into code. After the last investigation round, a person page that cites more than three mails and quotes fewer than six (ten past five mails), or has fewer than two Insight bullets, gets one more turn with nothing but the page and the originals it cites, told to put the exact words where the paraphrase is. If that turn loses a quote, it is undone. Across three reruns of five people the lowest page went from 3 quotes to 7 and the mean from 13.6 to 18.2; every page had at least two Insight bullets.

That turn first cost something we had just won: three of five pages lost their First contact again. The quote turn carried no facts item, so the audit refuted the computed date and nothing could restore it. A step that edits a page needs the same context as the step that wrote it.

Pronouns went the same way. The owner's rule is never to guess one, and pages kept writing "the user said he would". Code now replaces a he, his, she or her whose nearest named actor in the sentence is the user, and leaves alone any pronoun after another name or inside a quote. It misses some; it does not guess. Pronouns that would be read as the owner fell from 32 across fifteen pages to 1.

Daily upkeep had a different problem. Its notes, the only thing it reads, held no quotes, so no rule on the page could help. The extraction skill now puts a `Quote:` line in its examples instead of a paragraph at the end.

And a project page can now find its own history: sessions run in a temporary git worktree named after the work (feat-handoff) are filed with the project the name points to, not dropped as temporary.
