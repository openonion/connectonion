# 1.9.3b4: the context, not a task

*2026-10-15*

Aaron read a handoff the way its recipient would and stopped at the first line:
"Mia handed you a task." But that is rarely what people send. They send where a
piece of work got to: what they talked through, what they settled, what is
still open. Calling it a task tells the other person they now owe something,
before they have even seen what it is.

So the words changed wherever the recipient meets them first. The line they
paste says "handed you the context of", the mail's subject says "Handing you
the context of", and the button says "See the context I'm passing you". The
report keeps its Task section, because a handoff still says what the sender
would like done.

We sent one from a test sender to a Codex on another machine and pasted the
new line. Codex read it as before, accepted, and carried on.
