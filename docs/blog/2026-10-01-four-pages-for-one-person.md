# Four pages for one person

After a fresh `co rem init` on the owner's Mac, the second-busiest "person" in
the notebook was 常兴, with 108 mails. 常兴 is the owner. It is their other
Gmail account, aaronplus1996@, and the name their Mac account goes by.
aaron@openonion.ai had a person page too, and so did aaron.xie@mail.openonion.ai.
The owner's own page, meanwhile, listed neither.

The map had noticed. It asked, in init's summary and on the owner's page,
whether aaronplus1996@ and aaron@ were the owner's own, with the exact
`--mine` command to say so. That is by design. Months ago the owner's notebook
offered seventeen such addresses, and fifteen were friends who simply answer on
other channels, so the map asks rather than folds. But it asked and also made
the page. A question shown once, at the end of a long run, reads as a footnote.
The person page next to it reads as a fact. So the notebook said one thing and
showed another.

Now a possible own address is asked about and gets no page of its own. The
question stays where it was, on the owner's page with its command. Answering
it folds the address in, as before, and a page someone already wrote is never
touched. One more case is asked about too: an address that spells the owner's
full name, like aaron.xie@ for Aaron Xie, even when it has had replies,
because the owner's own aliases and agents answer mail as well.

The care was in what not to change. A map that knows nothing about the owner,
with no mailbox and no name, treats every address that is only written to as
"possibly yours". Applied there, the new rule would have left nobody the owner
writes to with a page at all, so it applies only when the map knows who the
owner is. And agent addresses stay people. An earlier decision kept them so,
since on this network an agent may well be someone else's.
