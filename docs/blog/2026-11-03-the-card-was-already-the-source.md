# The card was already the source

Elara Finch's fictional notebook page has an email address from a business card.
The address ends in `[1]`, and Sources says `1. Business card handed over at the
Lantern meetup`. The fact is there. Clicking its citation should explain where
it came from.

The reader expected `- [1] kind:target`. It discarded the numbered list label,
leaving a citation with nothing to open. Reinvestigating a card-only contact
would ask a different source to recover details it might never have held.

The fix reads the old label in place. Hovering shows the description; clicking
opens that text as source context. Typed sources still resolve their archived
originals. Page review recognizes the same definition without treating its
presence as proof that a claim is true. No notebook page needs rewriting.

Before the fix, the reader regression lost source 1 and page review reported its
citation missing. Both pass after the change. The useful lesson is small:
supporting an older source spelling can preserve evidence that a fresh search
cannot replace.
