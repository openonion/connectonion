# The deadline that went quiet

In a fictional notebook, Avery was still waiting for a draft requested in July.
While its October deadline was ahead, the reader kept it open. On the day it
was due, the item slipped into “Gone quiet.” Nothing had been delivered. The
calendar had simply crossed the boundary that stopped protecting it from aging.

The same assumption appeared in the writing instructions: a passed meeting date
or sixty days of silence could send a thread to History. Another sentence said
an overdue promise stayed open. Fixing the reader alone would leave its next
writer with contradictory instructions.

Recorded deadlines now stay in active counts regardless of their age. The
morning brief separates overdue items from those due soon. Older undated
threads keep their existing quiet grouping. Writers and source auditors need
evidence answering, delivering, cancelling or withdrawing the matching ask;
an elapsed date proves none of those things. An unanswered request also does
not become an agreed debt.

Before the repair, the focused regressions produced nine failures and four
passing controls. They exercise invented notebook text, the reader's JavaScript
and instructions dispatched to fake runners. They prove the broken boundary and
prompt contradiction, not a model's compliance or the status of any real promise.
