# Take the hedge, keep the evidence

A hedge on a page is a clause like "completion is unknown" or "no reply is recorded in the supplied mail", usually attached to a sentence that also holds a quote and two citations. Asked to remove the hedge, the editing turn removed the sentence. Told to put back what it removed, it put back the hedge too. On eight real pages with sixty hedges, the first left ten to twenty-one and lost up to eleven sources; the second lost nothing and left twenty-five to forty-three.

The clause is a small enough thing for code. A hedged line is split into its clauses; a clause about what is not known goes, unless it holds a quote, and its citations and privacy labels move to the end of the line. "No reply is recorded through 2026-10-20" is not a hedge at all once it is said plainly: it becomes "no reply as of 2026-10-20". Three reruns: twelve, twelve and eighteen hedges left of sixty, no quote or source lost.

The same day a people-page update was refused because the lines it put back made History seventeen lines long. History is folded again after anything is restored.
