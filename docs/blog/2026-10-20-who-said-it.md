# Who said it

We read every insight and quote the deepening rounds added to five pages, against the messages they cite. All thirty-four quotes were word-for-word accurate. Only four of the twelve insights were good: true, needing more than one message, and changing what the user would do next. Seven were weak and one was wrong.

The weak ones broke no rule we had written, which was the uncomfortable part. Muse already requires an insight to rest on two or more sources. The bullets met that by citing one email twice in different words, or by adding a second source that agreed with the first. So the test is now stated the way a reviewer would apply it: strike every citation but one. If the claim still stands, it is that one message's summary.

The wrong one replaced a correct bullet with its opposite. It read "release notices go to one place, campaigns to another" as a change of mind. Those are two kinds of content with two destinations. A "changed" bullet now needs the same subject before and after, and a new bullet may never replace one its own sources contradict.

The quotes failed in a different way: they were credited to the wrong speaker. A scheduler's prompt, an issue title and a client's ask forwarded by the owner were each written up as "the user asked". The speaker is now the original's author, and only a sentence carrying an ask, a decision, a commitment or a reason is quoted. One person page had forty-three mails behind it and quoted none of them. Enrich now puts what someone asked, decided or promised on the page in their own words.

Deepening also runs three pages at once per slot. A round spends most of its time waiting on model turns, and the owner would rather keep every turn of thinking than finish sooner.

We ran a second round on three pages that had already been deepened once. One project page ended with two "Now" bullets, the second a near-copy of the first, which put it at five bullets where four is the limit. Near-duplicate Insight bullets under the same label now merge the way same-date history lines do. One person page also named the notebook's owner by first name in a new bullet, where the rule is "the user". The owner's name, and the first name when nobody else in the notebook shares it, is now replaced outside quotes.
