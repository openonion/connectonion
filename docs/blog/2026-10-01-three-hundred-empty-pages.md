# Three hundred empty pages

The owner opened the notebook, clicked on people, and picked a few at random.
Every one was the same page: a name, an email address, a dozen headings each
followed by "Unknown — not investigated yet", and a History of one line,
"Observed mail count: 1". Their verdict was that the quality was poor wherever
they looked.

They were right, and for a reason no prompt could fix. Of 381 people pages,
374 had never been written by anything but the map. A page was made for
everyone who had sent or received a single mail: Apple, Xero, a real-estate
newsletter, six GitHub unsubscribe addresses, and 167 people the owner had
written to exactly once. Organisation pages followed their people, 141 of 142
just as empty. The investigation budget is about 30 pages a day. At that rate
the notebook would never catch up with its own map, and a page picked at random
would be a template.

We counted before choosing a rule. Of the 367 correspondents on the owner's map,
41 had mail going both ways and 31 more had been written to by the owner at
least twice. The other 295 were one message in one direction, or a newsletter
that never heard back. So a page is now for someone the owner corresponds
with: mail both ways, or the owner writing twice. Everyone else stays in the
map's records with their counts, and gets a page the day they reply.
Organisation pages already needed a person with a page, so most vendor domains
went with them.

`tidy` applies the same rule to notebooks made before it, moving map-only
pages into the archive without deleting them, and leaving any page someone or
something actually wrote. On a copy of the owner's notebook, people went from
381 to 85 and organisations from 142 to 18. What is left reads like a contact
list: Richard Lai, Ody Zhou, Tamara Berryman, Jiexuan Deng, Basem Suleiman.

One threshold moved with it. A nameless address the owner writes to and never
hears from is held for review rather than investigated, since it may be the
owner's own other mailbox. That used to take three sends. Now that two sends
earn a page, two sends are enough to hold it.
