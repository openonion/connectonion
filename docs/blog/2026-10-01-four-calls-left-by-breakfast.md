# Four calls left by breakfast

co rem runs on a schedule, and a cap keeps it from spending the owner's whole
day: thirty model calls. On the acceptance run of 1.9.0a6, the owner's notebook
had used twenty-six of them by six in the morning. A sync started by hand takes
about nine. So the first time the owner sat down and asked their notebook to
catch up, it refused.

The cap had been chosen when one call maintained one page and a night had a
few dozen sessions to read. Since then, the scheduled slots do more in the
early hours: they finish unfinished pages first and follow new mail. Each
change was right. Together they used the day's allowance before the day began.

There were honest choices: keep headroom for manual runs, run the schedule
less often, or raise the cap. They are a trade between background work and the
owner's own Codex week, which is the owner's to make, so we asked. The answer
was to raise it: 50 calls, with room for a manual sync or two after a full
night. A notebook still carrying the old default of 30 reads as 50; a value the
owner set by hand is kept.

The lesson: a limit chosen for one workload is a guess about every workload
after it. When the work changes, look at the limit again, and when the trade is
the user's money, let the user choose.
