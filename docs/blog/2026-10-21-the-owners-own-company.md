# The owner's own company

An organisation page is written from mail to and from the organisation's
domain. For a university that works: people there write to the owner. For the
owner's own company it does not. Mail to the company's addresses is mostly
receipts: package publishes, security alerts, billing. Last week's fix left
those out, and the page was left with nothing. Its best attempt also listed
the owner's own agent address as a colleague called "Aaron, a sender".

The company is the one organisation the owner writes about more than anyone
writes to them. So its page now comes from the owner's side. Once the owner's
page names their company, the company's page is gathered from what the owner
sent from every mailbox, the typed sessions in the projects the company owns,
and as leads only, the opening lines of its project pages and of the people and
organisations the owner deals with most. The writer is told what it is
looking at. This is the user's company. Say what it does, its products,
customers, team and live deals, in the user's own sent words with their dates.
Never list the user's addresses or agents as other people. Receipts are not
the company's work.

The map keeps the page too. It used to drop an organisation on the owner's own
domain entirely, so that the owner's company would never be mistaken for a
counterparty. It is still never a counterparty, but now it has a page.

A smaller fix went in with it. A Gmail server error, HTTP 500, ended an
organisation page's gathering and cost a 41-minute retry of the whole page.
Server errors 500 to 504 are now retried with a short backoff during
investigation and during mapping, the same way a dropped connection is.
